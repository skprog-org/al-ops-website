FAQ

Questions security and engineering ask.

The two reviews that decide whether AL Ops ships. Here's what both teams want to know first.

01Does AL Ops replace my orchestration framework, or wrap it?
AL Ops is the orchestration layer. Pipelines run on a LangGraph orchestrator with live Socket.IO state streaming, so you deploy multi-agent workflows directly — no separate runtime to babysit. If you already have agents, you connect them as steps and inherit gates, caps, and the audit chain.
02How are HITL approvals actually recorded?
Each approval gate captures the approver, decision, timestamp and context, signs it, and writes it to an immutable SHA-256 audit chain. Nothing lives in a Slack DM. When an auditor asks for the decision trail, you query it — you don't reconstruct it.
03Can token budgets really stop a runaway agent?
Yes — caps are enforced at run time, per agent and per pipeline, with overage alerts before the limit and a hard stop at it. Spend is attributed to cost centres, so the bill is never the first time you hear about a problem.
04What makes the DPDP / GDPR support "native"?
Consent, erasure, access and correction are workflows in the platform — wired to the same governance mesh that enforces your pipelines and data contracts — not a bolted-on consent banner. The mesh propagates a policy change to your API gateway, contracts, and approval gates simultaneously.
05Can we self-host or stay in our own cloud?
AL Ops deploys to your cloud with zero-trust defaults, encrypted secrets on every connector, SSO/SCIM and role-based access. Enterprise contracts include a dedicated environment and audit support. Talk to us if your security review is the long part.
06How long until the first audit-ready pipeline?
Most teams map their stack in week one via OAuth connectors and ship their first governed, audit-ready pipeline in week two — one sprint, no agents to hand-deploy.
