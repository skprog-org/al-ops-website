Trust Center

Move forward without losing accountability.

Business transformation involves decisions about information, people, and responsibility. Understand how those decisions are governed before changing how your organization works.

Information

Know how your information is handled.

Review data use, access, retention, and the responsibilities of each party. Confirm the terms that apply to your selected services and deployment.

Accountability

Keep people accountable for consequential decisions.

Define where review is required and who has authority to act. Make the boundaries part of the design, not an assumption.

Evidence

Review the evidence behind the commitments.

Access available security documentation, contractual terms, and assessment information. Check the scope and status of each assurance.

Assurance

Security claims should have a defined scope.

Each control statement should identify the service, deployment mode, and evidence it covers. Request the documents needed for your review rather than relying on a badge or a broad assurance.

Framework, not a representation. This Trust Center describes a control-design framework. It is not legal advice or a statement that the platform complies with any law or standard. Security, privacy and legal owners review the actual service, commitments and data flows before any statement becomes binding.

Evidence status

Every evidence item carries one of these statuses. A planned control is never shown as a completed assurance.

  • Available Issued and shareable under the stated access
  • Internal mapping Our own mapping, not an independent examination
  • In progress Work under way, not complete
  • Not available Does not exist for this scope
  • Pending confirmation Draft placeholder; not yet confirmed by the owner

What is public and what is shared under review.

High-level policies, status definitions and privacy information are public. Sensitive reports, detailed penetration-test findings and threat models are shared only through authenticated, authorized access.

A hidden page or a no-index tag is not access control, so sensitive documents are never published at a guessable address.
