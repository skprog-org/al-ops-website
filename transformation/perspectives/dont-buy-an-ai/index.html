Perspectives · Strategy

Do not buy an AI. Build the ability to change AI.

The model you choose this year may not be the model you run in three. The asset that lasts is the ability to adopt what comes next.

AL Ops · Published 30 September 2026

Most transformation plans start with a selection: which model, which platform, which vendor. It feels like the responsible first step. It is also where an enterprise begins to tie its future to the decision most likely to age quickly.

Technology choices age faster than programmes

Models improve, prices change, new providers appear, and capabilities that once needed custom engineering become standard. A plan that assumes today's leader will still be the right choice at delivery is betting against that pace.

The lasting asset is not the technology you chose. It is how cheaply you can choose again.

What lock-in looks like in practice

  • Applications call one provider's interface directly, so any change touches every application.
  • Prompts, retrieval indexes and workflows sit in a format that cannot be exported.
  • Governance lives inside one product, so leaving it means rebuilding your controls.
  • Skills concentrate around one vendor's tooling.

Build for change, then adopt quickly

The alternative is not to avoid new technology. It is to adopt it on a foundation that keeps each adoption reversible: a gateway between applications and models, open protocols, portable data and workflows, and governance built into the platform rather than into one tool.

With those in place, the question shifts from “which technology do we commit to?” to “which technology serves this workflow best today?”, and that question can be answered again each quarter.

A practical test

If a better model appeared next month, how long would it take to move one production workflow to it, and what would have to change? If the answer is months and touches applications, lock-in is accumulating, even if each decision looked sound.
