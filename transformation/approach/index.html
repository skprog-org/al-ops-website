Our approach

From business ambition to a change that works.

Start small enough to learn and important enough to matter. Agree the business result, establish the starting point, and make each next commitment on evidence.

Discuss a starting point

  1. Understand the business moment.

    What is changing? What is holding the business back? What opportunity becomes possible if that constraint is removed?

  2. Define the transformation.

    Describe the future experience or way of working. Set the scope, owner, expected value, risks, and conditions for success.

  3. Build and test the first move.

    Use the smallest credible implementation to test the business case. Include the people who will use it and the responsibilities needed to operate it.

  4. Scale what earns the right to scale.

    Review outcomes, adoption, and operating demands before expanding. Improve or stop approaches that do not deliver the agreed value.

Accountable, not absolute.

We define the result and measure the change. We do not guarantee transformation.

  • Every engagement starts with a business result and an agreed starting point.
  • Advisory, platform and implementation scope is written down, including what your team will own.
  • People stay accountable for consequential decisions; review points are part of the design.
  • Expansion is a decision made on evidence, not an assumption made at the start.

What the research says.

More companies are getting value from AI, but most still cannot see it in their profit. The difference is in leadership, workflows, people and controls more than in the models.

of 1,330 companies are future-built, with 2.3 times the shareholder return and 2.4 times the revenue growth of laggards. Another 41% are scaling AI.

The leaders start with clarity on where AI creates value, reshape workflows end to end, invest in governance and the workforce alongside the technology, and measure the impact in the P&L. Only 5% of companies have the full set of AI controls in place.

How we built this checklist.

Each check turns a practice that the research links to AI value into a statement you can verify in your own organization. The basis line shows where it comes from. Checks marked “Our practice” come from our own delivery work: the platform controls and ways of working we use to make those practices hold in production. They are our recommendation, not a finding from the studies.

The figures are from surveys of executives and reflect their own assessment of their companies. They show association, not cause. The EY survey covers US companies only, and McKinsey’s high-performer group is small (92 respondents). BCG, McKinsey and EY did not review or endorse this checklist.
