For technical evaluators

The platform stack.

Nine layers between your existing systems and the people who use them, with one control plane across all of them. Agents are run, supervised and recorded in one place, and people keep the decisions that matter.

Follow one decision through the stack

  1. Event arrives. A door camera reports torn stretch wrap on pallet LP-20417.
  2. Vision service scores the damage; confidence is low (0.61).
  3. Context is joined. The pallet is linked to its order, customer and put-away slot.
  4. Model gateway routes the call to an approved model for this data class.
  5. Policy-as-code requires a person for low-confidence exceptions.
  6. Supervisor decides. Inspects the load and overrules the re-wrap.
  7. Workbench records the decision and its reason on the supervisor’s tablet.
  8. Evidence record links the event, model call, policy and approval.
  1. Event arrives. The carrier’s ETA for door 4 slips by 25 minutes.
  2. What-if shows the 15:00 dispatch cut-off is at risk.
  3. Orchestrator proposes swapping doors 4 and 2.
  4. Policy-as-code checks yard rules and dock capabilities.
  5. Yard lead decides. Approves the swap.
  6. Messaging channel notifies the carrier of the new door.
  7. Evidence record captures the recommendation, approval and notice.

Every action leaves a linked record

Who asked, which agent, under whose authority, which data, which model, what it cost, who approved it and which control it satisfies. Records are chained so any alteration is visible (tamper-evident), and retained under locked policies.
