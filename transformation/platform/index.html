Right tool, right job

Not everything needs AI.

Some work needs only a simple automation or a script, and some systems are best left as they are. Real progress comes from finding where AI adds value and combining it with everything that already works.

Select one to see where it fits in a real process.

Example: from invoice to payment

  1. Keep

    The ERP stays the system of record for suppliers, orders and payments.

  2. Script

    A nightly script collects new invoices, orders and delivery notes into one place.

  3. Automate

    A matching rule clears every invoice that agrees with its order and delivery: most of them.

  4. AI

    AI reads the exceptions (a changed price, a partial delivery, a note in an email) and proposes a fix with its reasons.

  5. People decide

    The accounts owner approves the fix, and the ERP is updated through its normal interface.

Illustrative process. AI handles the exceptions, not the whole flow.

What building with AI has taught us.

  1. The best AI answer is often a script.

    Asked to do the same calculation a thousand times, a language model is the wrong tool. Asked to write the code that does it, it is the right one. AI should build the automation, not become it.

  2. Legacy systems usually hold the truth.

    Older systems encode years of rules and edge cases that nobody has written down. AI works best reading and acting through them, not replacing them.

  3. AI earns its place where the work is messy.

    Free text, unusual cases, conflicting information and decisions that need a summary of the trade-offs. That is where rules run out and judgment is needed.

  4. The progress is in the combination.

    Systems of record for truth, scripts and rules for what is predictable, AI for what is ambiguous, people for what is consequential. Each part does what it is best at, and the value shows up in the whole.

Modernising legacy systems

Decide each system’s future on purpose.

Keeping a legacy system should be a choice, not a default. We assess each system against what the business will need next, then choose one of five paths. Most estates use several at once.

  1. Retain

    Leave it as it is

    It is stable, cheap to run and rarely changes. Review it again when the business need changes.

  2. Wrap

    Connect around it

    The core is sound but hard to reach. Add interfaces so new services, automation and AI can use it safely.

  3. Extend

    Build next to it

    New needs keep arriving. Add them alongside, so the old core stops growing and new work is easier to change.

  4. Replace

    Move it step by step

    It holds the business back. Move one function at a time, with the old and new running together until each step is proven.

  5. Retire

    Switch it off

    Little depends on it any more. Move the remaining data and records, then stop paying for it.

Five questions behind the decision

  1. How much does the business depend on it, and what breaks if it stops?
  2. How often does it need to change, and how long does each change take?
  3. What does it cost to run, support and keep secure?
  4. Is the knowledge of how it works held by a few people, or written down?
  5. Does it block a result the business needs, such as a new service or real-time data?

Where AI helps modernisation

The hardest part of modernising is often understanding what the old system really does. AI can read old code, configuration and documents, explain the business rules hidden inside them, and draft the tests that prove a replacement behaves the same. People still decide what to keep, change or retire.

Technology principles

Engineered the way safety-critical industries work.

Automotive and life sciences never ship on trust: faults fail safe, processes are validated, and every batch leaves a record. The platform is built on the same principles.

Select a discipline to see what we brought back from it.

  1. From automotive

    Fail safe

    When the system is unsure, or the action is consequential, it stops and waits for a person.

  1. From life sciences

    Validated, not assumed

    Tested against its intended use, and retested whenever the model, data or rules change.

  1. From life sciences

    Traceable by design

    Every decision, source and approval leaves a record you can replay, like a batch record.

Principles that speed you up, not slow you down.

We help you ship on principle: built right the first time, so there is less rework, fewer surprises in production and faster approval from risk and compliance. Your business can keep pace with demand and stay ahead of competitors in a fast-moving market, without trading speed for safety.
